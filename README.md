# Project Altair – Science Sub-Team

**Author:** Areeba Zahra Kabir  

**Institution:** Islamic University of Technology (IUT), Bangladesh   

**Team:** Project Altair – Mars Rover Team

## Overview

This repository documents my Phase 1 and Phase 2 recruitment assessment submissions for the Science Sub-Team of Project Altair, the Mars Rover Team of the Islamic University of Technology.

The assessments involved exploring Martian geology, understanding the scientific principles behind biomolecule detection, investigating planetary habitability, selecting potential rover landing sites, and developing a conceptual scientific instrument for mineral analysis.

## Phase 1 – Scientific Research and Chemical Analysis

### 1. Martian Geological Feature Identification
Analyzed high-resolution Martian surface imagery to identify and interpret 23 potential geological and surface features, including:
- Aeolian landforms and sediment deposits
- Erosional structures and rocky outcrops
- Possible impact craters and surface depressions
- Geological formations and terrain variations

### 2. Chemical Testing and Biomolecule Detection
Investigated five qualitative chemical tests and evaluated alternative methods for detecting biological compounds.

The assessment covered:
- Chemical principles and reaction mechanisms
- Reagents and experimental procedures
- Laboratory safety considerations
- Advantages, disadvantages, and estimated costs
- Alternative tests for proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, and enzyme activity

### 3. Mars Habitability and Biosignatures
Explored the scientific objectives of Mars exploration, focusing on:
- Ancient water activity and planetary habitability
- Geological records and environmental conditions
- Biosignatures and potential evidence of past life
- The importance of geological context in scientific investigations

## Phase 2 – Mission Planning and Scientific Instrumentation

### 1. Mars Landing-Site Selection
Explored Athabasca Valles, Mars, to identify a potential rover landing-site area using Google Earth Pro and high-resolution planetary imagery.

Reference coordinates: 8.955° N, 155.085° E.

The assessment considered terrain characteristics, accessibility, geological features, and scientific importance.

### 2. Geological Hypotheses and Investigation Plan
Developed one primary scientific hypothesis and three related hypotheses concerning:
- Past volcanic activity
- Ancient water activity
- Potential preservation of biosignatures

Proposed suitable rock and soil samples and an investigation strategy for the simulated rover mission.

### 3. Conceptual Raman Spectrometer Design
Developed a conceptual 532 nm Raman spectroscopy system for mineral identification and analysis of Martian soil and rock samples.

The proposal included:
- Working principle of Raman spectroscopy
- Optical component selection and estimated costs
- Conceptual optical layout
- Rover science-payload integration
- Technical limitations and safety considerations

**Estimated conceptual budget: BDT 195,750.**
